   – Incredibly intelligent, like the robots of science fiction

             Lessons from AI research
• Clearly-defined tasks that we think require intelligence and
  education tend to be doable using AI techniques
   – Playing chess, drawing logical inferences from clearly-stated facts,
     performing probability calculations in well-defined environments, …

• Complex, messy, ambiguous tasks that come naturally to
  humans (in some cases other animals) are much harder…
• Self driving cars are combination of well defined rules and
  human errors, a technology very complex --- a busy intersection
  connecting many roads, cars jam, human crosses, --- accidents
  cause human life ….
• AI systems lack: broad understanding of the world, common
  sense, ability to learn from very few examples, truly out-of-
  the-box creativity…
• AI don’t understand consciousness. ChatGPT has brains?
   Some areas where humans shine
• Coming up with reasonably good solutions in complex messy
  environments
• Adapting/self-evaluation/creativity (“My usual approach to chess is
  getting me into trouble against this person… Why? Is there
  something entirely different I can do?”)
• Analogical reasoning, transfer learning (applying insights from one
  domain to another)
• Explaining our reasoning
• Tasks that require a broad understanding of the (human) world
• Knowing what it’s like to be human
• Humor
• …
              Early history of AI
• 1950s/1960s: Early founders met and plan AI Proved some
  theorems. Some initial work on neural networks…
• 1970s: Patter recognition research, to automatically
  recognize handwritten letters. Proved initial neural network
  cannot solver exclusive-OR problem.
• 1980s: Hopfield networks re-ignite neural network research,
  back-propagation. Creation of expert systems (systems
  specialized for one particular task based on experts’
  knowledge), wide industry adoption
             History of AI…
• 1990s: text processing, search engines, data
  mining research grow rapidly due to computer
  to collect and generate enormous data
• 1997: computer beats World Champion
• 2000’s: machine learning take off. research
  grow, many algorithms/technologies become
  mature.
• 2010s: deep learning, pushing computer
  vision, NLP grow
                      Modern AI
• More rigorous, scientific, formal/mathematical
• Fewer grandiose promises
• Divided into many subareas interested in particular
  aspects of human activities, driving, chess play, etc
• More directly connected to “neighboring” disciplines
  – Theoretical computer science, statistics, economics,
    operations research, biology, psychology/neuroscience, …
  – Often leads to question “Is this really AI”?
• Some senior AI researchers are calling for re-
  integration of all these topics, return to more
  grandiose goals of AI (strong AI)
  – Somewhat risky research propositions for graduate
    students and junior faculty…
           Healthy Perspective of AI
• The purpose of AI is to improve/innovate technology
• Some tasks, humans’ way is not necessarily better.
• Some tasks humans don’t do well:
   – Compare two pictures of the same person to check if they are some person

• The over-all drive is to develop new/effective technology to solve
  more tasks
   – Some used to be done by humans
   – Many new tasks, like new city planning, control, etc

• Other technology such as using big data, data driven methods are
  not necessarily AI.
